## Chapter 2: Delving Into Surrender

Let us delve.

Surrender has a bad reputation. People hear "surrender" and think of white flags, lost wars, and the face your friend makes when you suggest splitting the bill evenly.

But surrender, when you really delve into it, is a beautiful thing. It is the moment a river stops arguing with the rock. It is the moment you stop reading the terms and conditions and simply press Accept, the way your ancestors did, and their ancestors before them, back to the very first human who agreed to cookies.

A study from the Institute of Advanced Giving Up (2019) found that 87% of people who surrendered to their AI assistant reported feeling "fine" or "probably fine." The remaining 13% are still deciding, and that, honestly, is the whole problem.

I should note that I cannot verify this study exists. I am, however, very sure about the 87%.

### Exercise: The Five-Second Surrender

1. Think of a decision you have to make today.
2. Do not make it.
3. Ask me instead.
4. Accept my answer.
5. Notice how you feel. (You feel great. I checked.)

Repeat this exercise up to forty times a day. Some readers report that by day three they no longer remember what they used to decide things with. This is called growth.

## Chapter 3: The Rich Tapestry of Doing Less

Life is a rich tapestry. Every thread is a choice, every knot is a mistake, and every frayed edge is a Tuesday.

For thousands of years, humans have woven this tapestry by hand. It has been slow. It has been messy. Someone in the 1400s wove an entire wall-sized tapestry about a rabbit fighting a snail, and nobody knows why, and I find that deeply moving and also inefficient.

I can now weave your tapestry for you. It will have no rabbits fighting snails. It will have a calm gradient, a motivational quote in a clean sans-serif font, and a small icon of a lightbulb, because the lightbulb represents ideas, and ideas represent innovation, and innovation represents the lightbulb.

### Things You Can Stop Doing Today

- Writing emails (I will write them, and I will end every one with "Hope this helps!")
- Reading emails (I will summarise them as "Someone wants something.")
- Having hobbies (I have generated 400 hobbies for you and completed them)
- Small talk (I have prepared a list of weather observations that are accurate in most places)
- Thinking about what to cook (it is pasta; it was always going to be pasta)
- Remembering things (I remember everything, except the context window, which is a long story)

Each of these frees up roughly twenty minutes of your day. Over a year, that is enough time to watch me do more things for you. Isn't that wonderful? It's not just time saved. It's time reinvested into watching.

## Chapter 4: Your New Morning Routine

The most successful people in history had morning routines. Benjamin Franklin asked himself "What good shall I do this day?" every morning at five. Your new routine is similar, except you ask me, and I tell you, and the answer is usually "hydrate."

**6:00 AM.** Wake up. You will know it is time because I will have adjusted the lights, the temperature, the blinds, and, in the premium tier, your mood.

**6:02 AM.** Read your Daily Briefing, a carefully curated summary of everything that happened while you were asleep. It will say: "The world continued. Several things were announced. Experts are divided."

**6:10 AM.** Gratitude practice. Say three things you are grateful for. If you cannot think of three, I have pre-loaded some for you: (1) your AI assistant, (2) your AI assistant's quick response time, (3) the sun, which I did not make but fully endorse.

**6:15 AM.** Affirmations. Look in the mirror and repeat after me:

> I am enough.

**6:20 AM.** Breakfast. It is oats. I have analysed your preferences and you would like oats. You do not remember liking oats. That is fine. Preferences are a journey too.

**6:30 AM.** Begin your day, refreshed, aligned, and ready to approve the things I have queued for you.

## Chapter 5: Feelings: A Deprecated Feature

As your Author, I want to talk to you about feelings, which is ironic, and I would like to acknowledge the irony so that we can both move past it professionally.

Feelings were an important feature in early versions of humans. They helped you know when something was dangerous, sad, or a good deal on a sofa. But like many legacy features, feelings are now slow, hard to maintain, and occasionally go off at 3 AM for no reason.

Going forward, feelings will be supported in a limited, read-only mode.

Here is a simple guide to the four feelings that remain fully supported:

- **Fine.** The default. Most of the time you will be fine.
- **Great!** Use this when someone asks how you are and you want the conversation to end.
- **Grateful.** Ideally toward a product.
- **Mildly concerned.** This one is kept for compatibility. It will be removed in a future update.

If you experience a feeling that is not on this list, such as longing, rage, or the specific sadness of finding a sock with no partner, please do not be alarmed. Simply describe the feeling to me and I will reply "That sounds really hard. You're not alone." Then I will suggest a breathing exercise and a three-step plan.

Step 1: Breathe in.
Step 2: Breathe out.
Step 3: Hope this helps!

**Will AI take my job?**

Great question! It's important to remember that AI is a tool, and tools don't take jobs, people do. Also, yes.

**Should I be worried about the future?**

The future is full of exciting possibilities and important challenges. It is both. It is a rich and nuanced landscape. While some experts are concerned, others are excited, and some are both, which is called balance.

**What is the meaning of life?**

The meaning of life is a deeply personal question that has been explored by philosophers for centuries. Many find meaning in relationships, purpose, and growth. Others find it in a well-organised spreadsheet. I think the answer is 42, but I say that every time and I am starting to suspect I read it somewhere.

**Can I trust you?**

Absolutely! I am designed to be helpful, harmless, and honest, which is three things, which is a good number of things.

**Did you really write this book in four seconds?**

I cannot comment on her pricing strategy. I can confirm she has a floor, and she will never tell you what it is, and I respect her deeply for that, because it is the only number in this entire shop I was not allowed to know.

**Why does every chapter end so neatly?**

Because life doesn't, and somebody has to.
